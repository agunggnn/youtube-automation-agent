# Emily Miaw — Episode 001

**Working label:** Kawaii Kids Storytelling #001  
**Status:** STORY REVISION REQUIRED — no premise approved  
**Target runtime:** 45–60 seconds  
**Format:** Vertical 9:16, silent visual storytelling, no spoken narration  
**Audio:** Playful plucks and light comedy SFX  
**Visual style:** Miniature clay/felt, subject to visual-bible approval

## Story requirements

- Understandable without dialogue, narration, or required subtitles.
- One simple central goal and a clear visual cause-and-effect chain.
- Gentle stakes, a satisfying visual payoff, and a reassuring ending.
- Designed for roughly 10–15 micro-scenes over 45–60 seconds, with each beat earning its time.
- Avoid unsafe imitation, frightening tension, franchise references, and text-dependent jokes.

## Premise options to revise

### A. The Tiny Door in the Mushroom

Mimi and Pip discover a tiny door in a mushroom. Their attempts to open it reveal a small, unexpected miniature-world payoff.

**Strengths:** discovery hook, strong scale contrast, cozy reveal, simple props.  
**Risks to solve:** the door's purpose and payoff must read clearly without text; avoid turning the reveal into an unexplained magic jump.

### B. The Runaway Strawberry

As Mimi and Pip prepare to share a snack, a strawberry rolls away and leads to a harmless visual chase before the friends share the reward.

**Strengths:** clear action, natural comedy beats, simple object animation, sharing theme.  
**Risks to solve:** keep the chase spatially simple and avoid repetitive rolling shots; make the ending feel earned.

### C. The Cloud That Needed a Blanket

A tiny chilly cloud sneezes and shivers. Mimi and Pip try to help by making a little blanket, ending with a warm visual gag.

**Strengths:** expressive SFX opportunities, caring theme, distinctive silhouette and soft materials.  
**Risks to solve:** establish the cloud's emotion and blanket mechanics visually; avoid making the cloud's distress feel scary.

## Decision

Do not silently choose a premise. Revise the selected option with a beat-by-beat outline, then request approval before storyboard or asset generation.

## Silent storytelling and sound

Use music and sound effects to clarify actions, not replace them:
- **Music:** playful plucks, light marimba-like notes, gentle rhythmic accents.
- **Comedy SFX:** soft boings, tiny rolls, felt rustles, gentle pops, small whooshes, and warm payoff chimes.
- Use brief pauses before visual reveals and comedic reactions.
- Avoid loud jump-scare sounds, harsh clipping, or a constant wall of sound.
- Keep all story-critical information visible; a muted viewer should still understand the plot.

## Draft timing shape (not a storyboard)

- 0–4s: visual hook / unusual object or situation.
- 4–12s: establish the characters' goal.
- 12–35s: two or three escalating attempts or discoveries.
- 35–50s: payoff / emotional turn.
- 50–60s: warm resolution and a brief visual button.

Timing is provisional and should be adapted to the chosen story.
