# Emily Miaw — Character Concepts

**Status:** Preliminary concepts for approval. No character artwork has been generated.

The names, silhouettes, accessories, and behavior below are proposed original designs. The final model sheets and image references require explicit approval before generation.

## Mimi — The Gentle Dreamer

- **Species / silhouette:** small, round bunny with a soft cream/ivory felt body.
- **Signature feature:** long, floppy ears that communicate emotion.
- **Accessory:** a simple mint bow.
- **Face:** minimal expressive eyes, small nose, subtle blush cheeks.
- **Material:** tactile wool felt with visible fine fibers; understated handmade seams.
- **Personality:** gentle, observant, caring, quietly curious.
- **Signature movement:** ears lift when curious, then droop softly when surprised or uncertain.
- **Story function:** notices details and helps the audience understand the emotional stakes.

### Expression set

Happy, Curious, Worried, Surprised, Proud, Confused, Oops, Determined.

Keep the same eye spacing, muzzle, head-to-body ratio, ear attachment points, and bow placement across expressions.

## Pip — The Little Inventor

- **Species / silhouette:** small, round lavender kitten with short triangular ears and a comma-shaped tail.
- **Signature features:** expressive ears and a small star-shaped hair clip.
- **Accessory:** yellow canvas satchel.
- **Face:** simple expressive eyes and a small, readable mouth.
- **Material:** soft felt body; matte clay details and accessories.
- **Personality:** curious, optimistic, inventive, occasionally overconfident in a harmless way.
- **Signature movement:** raises one paw when an idea appears; ears react before the rest of the body.
- **Story function:** initiates playful experiments and gentle comic complications.

### Expression set

Happy, Curious, Worried, Surprised, Proud, Confused, Oops, Determined.

Keep the same head shape, ear angles at rest, tail silhouette, clip position, and satchel design across expressions.

## Duo dynamics

- Mimi tends to notice and care; Pip tends to try and experiment.
- Neither character should be reduced to a constant “smart one” or “silly one”; both learn and help.
- Humor comes from timing, reactions, and harmless surprises—not cruelty or distress.
- Endings should reinforce warmth, cooperation, discovery, or sharing.
